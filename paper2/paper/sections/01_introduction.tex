\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Let $X$ be a smooth toric Fano variety of dimension $n$. We study slope
stability of its tangent bundle with respect to $H=-K_X$. The tangent
bundle of a nontrivial product is not stable. Toric bundles and invariant
blowups of products can have stable tangent bundles, although either
construction can also admit a proper tangent subsheaf of excessive slope.
We give stability criteria for two classes of these varieties, with
unbounded dimension and Picard number.

For an invariant prime divisor $D_\rho$, write
\[
 \delta_\rho=\frac{nD_\rho H^{n-1}}{H^n}.
\]
These positive weights sum to $n$. The toric slope criterion states that
the tangent bundle is stable if and only if
\[
 \sum_{u_\rho\in V}\delta_\rho<\dim V
\]
for every nonzero proper subspace spanned by primitive ray generators
\cite{Klyachko1990,HeringNillSuess2022}. For the blowups considered here,
we reduce these inequalities to one divisor-degree inequality at each
vertex of a tree and express its terms as mixed intersections on the
blowup centres. For the toric bundles, we determine the largest Picard
number compatible with stability, allowing arbitrary integral twists.
We construct bundles attaining this maximum from every fixed tree of
maximum degree three, after sufficiently long independent subdivisions
of its edges.

\subsection*{Blowups of products of projective spaces}
Let $G$ be a finite simple graph and choose integers $b_v\ge1$. Start
with $\prod_v\PP^{b_v}$ and assign distinct coordinate hyperplanes at
each factor to its incident edges. For each edge $\{v,w\}$, blow up
the codimension-two centre defined by its two assigned hyperplanes.
These star subdivisions commute. The resulting variety $Y(G,b)$ is
Fano exactly when $b_v\ge d_v:=\deg_G(v)$ for every $v$, and then
\[
 \dim Y(G,b)=\sum_v b_v,\qquad
 \rho(Y(G,b))=|V(G)|+|E(G)|.
\]
At each vertex there is an unassigned hyperplane. Its inverse image
has divisor class $D_v=f_v^*\mathcal O_{\PP^{b_v}}(1)$, where $f_v$
is the original factor projection composed with the blowdown. Set
$A_v=nD_vH^{n-1}/H^n$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mixed-intro}
If $G$ is a tree with at least one edge and $b_v\ge d_v$ at every
vertex, then $T_{Y(G,b)}$ is anticanonically stable if and only if
$A_v<1$ for every $v$. It is semistable if and only if $A_v\le1$
for every $v$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{mixed:tree} reduces the slope inequalities for subspaces
meeting any number of factors to the stated vertex inequalities.
At a nonleaf vertex with
$b_v=d_v$, the distinguished ray defines a rank-one foliation with
first Chern class $D_v$. Its degree satisfies the identity
\[
 (b_v+1)A_v=b_v+\sum_{e\ni v}J_{v,e}.
\]
Each $J_{v,e}$ is a positive first moment of the corresponding
exceptional facet. Section~\ref{sec:intrinsic} expresses this moment
as a mixed intersection on the blowup centre. The factor projection
specifies an ordering of the two normal directions. Moreover,
\[
 J_{v,e}+J_{w,e}=\frac{nE_eH^{n-1}}{H^n},\qquad
 \sum_{e\ni v}J_{v,e}
 =\frac{nf_v^*(-K_{\PP^{b_v}})H^{n-1}}{H^n}-b_v.
\]
The stability inequality for this foliation therefore depends on the
sum of the exceptional contributions at the vertex. The mixed-intersection
formula also applies to compatible blowup morphisms whose incidence
graph is not a tree.

Call a vertex \emph{saturated} when $b_v=d_v$.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:mixed-trees-intro}
Let $G$ be a tree with at least one edge and maximum degree at most
three. If every nonleaf vertex is saturated and the leaf dimensions
are arbitrary positive integers, then $T_{Y(G,b)}$ is anticanonically
stable. A saturated degree-four vertex adjacent to four
saturated degree-four vertices forces instability, independently of
the farther branches of a Fano tree.
\end{theorem}

The assertions are proved in Theorems~\ref{treemix:subcubic}
and~\ref{treemix:degree-four}. The stable case allows arbitrary distances
between branching vertices and imposes no symmetry assumption. A saturated tree
with $M$ edges has dimension $2M$ and Picard number $2M+1$.
Thus maximum degree three is the largest degree bound that guarantees
stability for every saturated tree. Higher degrees can occur in stable
examples: there are stable trees with two branching vertices of degree
four or five and arbitrarily long connecting paths
(Proposition~\ref{treemix:two-centres}). Their exceptional first moments
depend on the dimensions of neighbouring factors and the arrangement
of the branches.

\subsection*{Toric bundles with maximal Picard number}
Let $S_6$ denote the blowup of $\PP^2$ at its three torus-fixed points.
Consider locally trivial toric bundles with fibre $S_6^j$ over
$\prod_{c=1}^k\PP^{b_c}$, where $j,k,b_c\ge1$, in the fan
presentation~\eqref{interact:rays}. The twists are arbitrary integral
vectors in the fibre lattice.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}\label{thm:bundlebound}
For every $n\ge4$, the largest Picard number among smooth toric Fano
$n$-folds in this bundle class with anticanonically stable tangent
bundle is
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{5n}{4}\right\rfloor+1.
\]
\end{theorem}

For each base factor, the Fano condition bounds the number of nonzero
twists by the dimension of that factor. A slope inequality for the
relative tangent bundle requires each surface factor to have nonzero
twists from at least two base factors. Stability further requires the
incidence graph of these twists to be connected. Counting its edges
gives the bound in Theorem~\ref{interact:bound}. Bundles indexed by paths
attain it in dimension $4j$, and the end attachments of
Corollary~\ref{attach:sharp} give the remaining dimensions.

For a tree $\Gamma$, assign one degree-six del Pezzo surface factor to
each edge and a base factor $\PP^{\deg(v)}$ to each vertex. Give each
surface factor opposite primitive-ray twists at the two endpoints of
its edge, as in Definition~\ref{graph:construction}, and
denote the bundle by $X(\Gamma)$.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:tree-intro}
Every fixed finite tree of maximum degree three with at least one
edge gives stable $T_{X(\Gamma)}$ after replacing each edge by an
independently chosen sufficiently long path. A sufficient minimum
length depends only on the number $s$ of trivalent vertices and is
$O(\log s)$ as $s\to\infty$. If $\Gamma$ has $j$ edges, then
\[
 \dim X(\Gamma)=4j,\qquad \rho(X(\Gamma))=5j+1.
\]
For each integer $q\ge1$, every sufficiently large $j$ admits at least
$q$ pairwise nonisomorphic toric fans of such stable extremizers.
\end{theorem}

The subdivision assertion is Theorem~\ref{tree:stable}. The graph can
be recovered from the toric fan, so nonisomorphic graphs yield pairwise
nonisomorphic fans (Lemma~\ref{graph:reconstruction} and
Corollary~\ref{tree:abundance}). This gives the asserted abundance,
without classifying all bundles attaining the bound. The proof uses
exponential contraction of the functions that give volumes of polytope
slices, together with a rank formula for subspaces meeting several
branches. Figure~\ref{fig:two-constructions} compares the bundle and
blowup constructions. For the blowups, subdivision can destroy
stability (Corollary~\ref{branch:degree-five}).

\subsection*{Relation to earlier work}
The toric subspace criterion and its formulation in terms of
cone-volume concentration are due to
\cite{Klyachko1990,HeringNillSuess2022}. Hering--Nill--S\"u\ss{}
determine stability regions for smooth toric surfaces and varieties
of Picard rank two. Dasgupta--Dey--Khan independently treat Picard
rank two and classify the smooth toric Fano fourfolds of Picard rank
three \cite{DasguptaDeyKhan2020}; the Picard-rank-at-most-two
fourfold case is due to Biswas--Dey--Genc--Poddar
\cite{BiswasDeyGencPoddar2021}. Reynolds treats all $124$ smooth toric
Fano fourfolds and their unstable Harder--Narasimhan filtrations
\cite{Reynolds2023}. Devey develops finite matroid tests for
equivariant reflexive sheaves \cite{Devey2022}. We use saturated
subsheaves, as required by the erratum \cite{DasguptaDeyKhan2021}.
The single-edge blowup with dimensions $(1,3)$ is already stable by
\cite[Proposition~5.6.1(2)]{DasguptaDeyKhan2020}.

The families studied here have no bound on the dimension or the number
of factors. For blowups, we prove the reduction to vertex inequalities,
the exceptional mixed-intersection identity, and uniform estimates
that establish stability or instability under the stated graph and
dimension hypotheses. These estimates use
Efron's monotonicity theorem through
\cite[Theorems~3.2--3.3]{saumardwellner2018}.
For bundles, the relative tangent slope inequality follows from a divergence
identity related to \cite[Lemma~2.2 and Eq.~(4.4)]{HenkLinke2014}.
Although the unweighted fibre polytope is centred, weighting it by the
volumes of the base slices can give a nonzero first moment. This first
moment enters the relative tangent slope calculation. We use the fixed polarization $-K_X$;
results for polarizations near a pulled-back or contracted class
\cite{NapameTipler2024,ClarkeNapameTipler2025} do not give the needed
inequalities. The preceding paper \cite{Wuebben2026} constructs stable
root-twisted bundles with $\rho=n+2$; allowing the number of surface
factors to grow produces the larger extremum and branching families here.

The bundle existence proofs use finite exact rational inequalities as
premises for arguments covering all lengths. These arguments use the
Birkhoff--Hopf contraction theorem
\cite{EvesonNussbaum1995}, uniform error estimates, and induction.
The appendices give the matrices, inequalities, and error estimates;
the ancillary code reproduces the finite calculations. The blowup
proofs use integral identities, comparisons, and induction, with
explicit polynomial arithmetic. Appendix~\ref{sec:computation} gives
classification calculations, including the unique stable eightfold
of Picard number eleven.

After the common stability criterion, Sections~\ref{sec:mixed}--\ref{sec:mixed-tree-threshold}
develop the blowup results. Sections~\ref{sec:interactions}--\ref{sec:subdivided}
treat the bundle bound and the construction of families attaining it.
The appendices contain the path, end-attachment, and three-arm
calculations, as well as further families and classification data.
The Picard maxima proved here concern the two specified construction
classes. Section~\ref{sec:questions} discusses the open problem of the
unrestricted maximum.
